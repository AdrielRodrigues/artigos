# Routing and dynamic core allocation with fragmentation optimization in EON-SDM

#### A R T I C L E I N F O

*Keywords:* Heuristic approach Contiguous aligned slot ratio (CASR) Bandwidth blocking probability (BBP) Fragmentation coefficient (FC) Crosstalk (XT) Dynamic core allocation

#### A B S T R A C T

The increasing need for rapid data transmission in optical networks has made the effective allocation of spectrum resources a crucial necessity. This study presents a new spectrum allocation approach that incorporates path selection and careful consideration of cross-talk (XT) limitations. We propose a novel fragmentation coefficient and dynamic core-changing-based routing, spectrum, and core allocation (FC-DCC-RSCA) technique. The algorithm begins by searching for the shortest paths using the Dijkstra algorithm. It then proceeds to evaluate fragmentation coefficients (FC) using the Continuous Aligned Slot Ratio (CASR) model. Subsequently, the FC undergoes a filtration process wherein only those falling inside predetermined fragmentation ranges (min and maxFC) are retained. Further spectrum allocation has been done based on XT constraints. The algorithm has better proficiency in dynamic flexibility, enabling it to effectively handle varying traffic loads. By conducting a series of rigorous simulations, our proposed approach exhibits a notable decrease in bandwidth blocking probability (BBP) while also opening the door for considerable improvements in spectrum utilization. The algorithm's proficiency in adapting to changing network circumstances signifies its importance as a helpful thing in enhancing the allocation of resources within optical networks. It is wellpositioned to address the developing requirements of contemporary communication systems. The spectrum utilization of the FC-DCC-RSCA increases by 12.5% and 6.25%, exhibits a reduction in its FR by 13.5% and 2.7%, and has a BBP of 0.051 for the traffic load of 450 Erlang, compared to SBP-PMC and FMDE-RSCA. The algorithm that has been provided provides opportunities for more investigation and enhancement. Potential areas for future research include the examination of strategies to improve energy efficiency, the development of scalable solutions for bigger network infrastructures, and the incorporation of machine learning methodologies to facilitate dynamic decision-making processes. Furthermore, it is worth investigating the potential of the method to be applied in developing optical communication technologies, such as space-division multiplexing. These endeavors hold the potential to enhance the current level of knowledge and practices in the field of optical network management and optimization.

# **1. Introduction**

In recent years, the elastic optical network (EON) has been intensively researched and is used as a remedy for the inefficient spectrum utilization problem of conventional wavelength division multiplexing (WDM) optical networks [[1](#page-11-0)[,2\]](#page-11-1). In conventional WDM networks, the channel bandwidth is fixed at 50 GHz, which is incompatible with the connection requests of clients with varying bandwidth requirements [\[3\]](#page-11-2). For low-data-rate connections where only a small portion of the channel is utilized for the allocation, a 50 GHz channel must be allocated entirely while only a small portion of the channel is utilized for allocation [\[4\]](#page-11-3).

EON is a highly promising alternative for the establishment of highcapacity networks. In the present day, within the framework of EON, it is possible to partition bandwidth into discrete frequency slots (FS), which facilitate the transmission of data at high rates. When initiating a new connection, the connection specifies its necessary bandwidth by quantifying it in terms of the number of frequency slots (FS). The connection will be accepted and then established if the network can designate the requested FS in a continuous pattern for each link of the selected route. Otherwise, access will be denied [[5](#page-11-4)[–7\]](#page-11-5). EON utilizes subcarriers that possess a significantly narrower frequency slot bandwidth (12.5 GHz) in comparison to traditional fixed-grid WDM networks. The use of a network facilitates the establishment of lightpath connections through the careful selection of an appropriate quantity of closely positioned subcarriers. #### 3.1. Network model

EON-SDM is represented by a network topology-based connectivity graph G (V, E, C, SS), in which V represents the number of nodes and E represents the fiber link set. C represents the collection of cores on a link, and its notation is  $C = C_1, C_2, \ldots, C_n$ . Each fiber core in a link carries a certain number of frequency slots, or SS. We have implemented the proposed algorithm using MATLAB. Initially, a network graph is constructed. Following this, an adjacency matrix, which functions as a binary map indicating the connections between these nodes, is defined. A value of 1 in the matrix signifies a connection, while 0 denotes no direct link. Connection Requests: A new connection request (NCR) is denoted as (S, D, SS), where S represents the source node, D represents the destination node, and SS represents the requested spectrum slice. This indicates a request to establish a light path between two nodes with a specified spectrum requirement.

**Path Selection (Algorithm 1):** The algorithm begins by using the Dijkstra algorithm to identify the k-shortest paths between the source and destination nodes. These paths depict possible connections for the light path.

Core Selection (Algorithm 2): After determining the k-shortest paths, the algorithm selects a suitable core for the connection. Based on CASR, the FC of each path is evaluated. CASR assesses the availability of spectrum channels that are contiguous on a core. Paths that do not satisfy the predefined FC constraints (minFC and maxFC) are discarded, and the remaining paths are classified based on their FC values. The path with the lowest FC value and its associated core are chosen.

Spectrum Slice Assignment with XT Constraint and Dynamic Core Selection (Algorithm 3): The model attempts to allocate the requested spectrum slice (SS) while adhering to the XT (crosstalk) level constraint with the given path. This step ensures that the allotted SS does not cause excessive interference or crosstalk with the adjacent slices. If the XT constraint is satisfied, that is, if  $XT < XT_{threshold}$ , the SS is allocated; otherwise, the algorithm seeks iteratively for an accessible SS by evaluating alternative paths and cores as needed. If the XT constraint is not satisfied, the model includes a provision for dynamically altering the core to allocate spectrum using alternative cores. This adaptability permits the network to accommodate varying conditions and constraints, ensuring successful SS allocation while minimizing crosstalk. # **6. Conclusion and future work**

<span id="page-11-10"></span>This research introduces a unique paradigm for spectrum allocation that aims to effectively manage light path demands in optical networks. The algorithm being presented utilizes a heuristic methodology that incorporates path selection and XT constraint analysis in order to optimize the allocation of spectrum resources while simultaneously minimizing both the chance of bandwidth blockage and fragmentation. Next, we assess FC using the CASR model, where we use a filtering and prioritization process to identify the pairs that fall between the predetermined minFC and maxFC limits. The flexibility of our algorithm to adapt to changing network circumstances and fluctuating demand patterns is a significant advantage. By using dynamic allocation of processing cores and looking at different routing options, our solution shows that it can work even when the amount of traffic changes. The FC-DCC-RSCA demonstrates an improvement in spectrum utilization by 12.5% and 6.25%, along with a reduction in its FR by 13.5% and 2.7%. Additionally, it displays a BBP of 0.051 for a traffic load of 450 Erlang when compared to SBP-PMC and FMDE-RSCA. Future research endeavors may focus on conducting further enhancements to the algorithm, taking into account supplementary variables such as energy efficiency and scalability. This will enable the continuous progression of the current state-of-the-art in the realm of optical network management.
