# Reading notes

This is where I write my own interpretation of a paper — not a distillation of what it says (that's `md/*_summary.md` and `concepts.md`), but my position on it: what I believe, what it connects to, why it matters to me.

One file per paper: `01_notes/<paper-name>.md`, using the same base filename as the paper's `pdf`/`md`/`_summary.md` so it's trivial to find.

## Template

```markdown
# <Paper Title>

**Read:** YYYY-MM-DD · **Topic folder:** <topic>/

## One paragraph, in my own words
(Not the abstract restated — what I'd tell a colleague in 30 seconds.)

## The actual question they're answering
(Often narrower than the title suggests. What specific problem, under
what constraints?)

## What convinced me / what didn't
- Strongest evidence:
- Weakest link in the argument (assumption, missing comparison, small-scale eval):

## How this connects
- Agrees with / builds on: [other-paper]
- Contradicts or complicates: [other-paper]
- Concept it deepens: [[concept-name in concepts.md]]

## Why it matters to me
(Which of my own questions does this move forward, or close off?)

## Open questions I'm left with
-

## Worth re-reading if:
(the one condition under which I'd come back to this paper specifically)
```

## Later: an index page

Once there are enough notes (roughly 8-10) to actually connect, it's worth building a single index page — not a file listing, but a map of the questions/claims I actually care about, each linking to the notes that bear on it. That's the point where notes stop being N separate book reports and start being a body of understanding. Not worth building before there's material to organize.
