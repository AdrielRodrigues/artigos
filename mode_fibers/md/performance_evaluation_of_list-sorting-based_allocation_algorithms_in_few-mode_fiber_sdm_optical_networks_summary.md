# Performance Evaluation of List-sorting-based Allocation Algorithms in Few-Mode Fiber SDM Optical Networks

## I. Introduction
To address the capacity limits of traditional Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM) networks, this paper explores Mode-Group Division Multiplexing (MGDM) using Few-Mode Fiber (FMF). MGDM allows multiple signals to transmit simultaneously via different spatial mode-groups within a single fiber. However, practical implementation requires solving the Routing, Modulation, Mode-Group, and Wavelength Allocation (RMMWA) problem to manage physical impairments like crosstalk and differential mode delay.

The authors evaluate two new sorting-based allocation algorithms against a baseline called BANG (Balanced AllocatioN of Mode-Group). While BANG uses a fixed order—prioritizing highest-capacity Mode-Groups and iterating through modulation formats from longest to shortest reach—the proposed algorithms prioritize either optical reach or transmission capacity to better align with specific request requirements.

## II. Models

### A. Physical layer model
The network utilizes MGDM-WDM, where channels are defined as a wavelength supporting a specific Mode-Group (MG). The model accounts for various physical impairments including attenuation, dispersion, and the Kerr effect. 

Key physical characteristics include:
- **Mode-Groups ($G$):** Ordered from lower-order to higher-order. Lower-order groups experience less intermodal interference, resulting in longer optical reach but lower capacity. Higher-order groups provide more capacity (due to more LP modes) but have shorter reach.
- **Modulation Formats ($M$):** A set of formats ordered by their efficiency relative to reach.
- **Resource Pairs ($A$):** The combination of a Mode-Group and modulation format defines the specific optical reach ($R_{m,g}$) and transmission capacity ($C_{m,g}$). 

### B. Network and traffic models
The network is represented as a graph $\Gamma = (N, L)$ with unidirectional links of equal capacity. Traffic consists of connection requests $(src, dst, b)$, where $b$ is the requested bitrate selected from a predefined set $B$. Both node selection and bitrate selection are uniform. Request arrivals and holding times follow exponential distributions, with network load measured in Erlangs ($\lambda/\mu$).

## III. Sorted-Based Algorithms Proposals
The authors propose two algorithms—Reach-Sorted and Rate-Sorted—designed to optimize resource use through a two-stage process:

**1. Offline Stage:** 
A `sorted_list` is generated by sorting all Mode-Group/modulation pairs in descending order based on either optical reach (for Reach-Sorted) or capacity (for Rate-Sorted). Additionally, the K-shortest paths for all node pairs are precomputed.

**2. Online Stage (RMMWA Process):**
When a request arrives:
- **Routing:** The algorithm iterates through the K-shortest paths.
- **Modulation and Mode-Group Selection:** For a given path and bitrate $b$, the `sorted_list` is filtered to create a subset (`AUX_M`) containing only pairs that satisfy the minimum capacity requirement ($C_{m,g} \ge b$) and whose reach exceeds the route length ($R_{m,g} \ge$ route length). The first available pair from this list is selected.
- **Wavelength Allocation:** A First Fit approach is used to assign a wavelength for that Mode-Group across all links in the path.

The algorithmic complexity is $O(K \cdot M \cdot G \cdot W \cdot L)$. 

**Comparison of Sorting Strategies:**
- **Mode-Group Sorted (BANG/Baseline):** Iterates by highest capacity MG first, then through modulation formats from longest reach to shortest.
- **Reach-Sorted:** Prioritizes pairs with the absolute maximum optical reach.
- **Rate-Sorted:** Prioritizes pairs with the highest transmission data rate.

## IV. Simulation Results
The algorithms were tested using the C++ Flex Net Sim simulator on two topologies: NSFNet (medium connectivity, mixed link lengths) and UKNet (higher connectivity, shorter links). Three Mode-Groups and three modulation formats were used, with 81 wavelengths per group.

**Key Findings:**
- **NSFNet Topology:** Both Reach-Sorted and Rate-Sorted outperformed the baseline. Reach-Sorted was particularly effective at low-to-medium loads because NSFNet's mixed link lengths make reach-based prioritization critical for high-bitrate demands. The maximum relative improvement over the baseline was 21%.
- **UKNet Topology:** Rate-Sorted performed best, especially at mid-to-high loads, achieving a significant blocking probability reduction of up to 77% at low loads and 9% at high loads compared to the baseline. This is attributed to UKNet's shorter links and higher connectivity, which shift the bottleneck from optical reach to available capacity.

## V. Conclusion
The study demonstrates that resource allocation efficiency in MGDM-WDM networks depends heavily on network topology. Reach-based prioritization (Reach-Sorted) is superior for networks with longer or mixed-length links (like NSFNet), while capacity-based prioritization (Rate-Sorted) is more effective for highly connected networks with shorter links (like UKNet). The authors conclude that tailored sorting strategies significantly reduce blocking probabilities compared to fixed Mode-Group sorting and suggest the development of adaptive algorithms that can switch strategies in real time based on network metrics.
